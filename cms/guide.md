# Your site guide

Everything you might want to change on your website is in this editor, and this guide walks through all of it. It always lives at the top of the menu, so you never have to go looking for it.

## The basics

- **Getting in:** go to app.pagescms.org, click **Sign in with GitHub**, and open **PinterestMarketing**. Bookmark that page so it's one click next time.
- **Nothing goes live until you click Save.** Type as much as you like, then save when you're happy.
- **After you save, your site updates on its own in about two minutes.** Refresh your site to see it. If it still looks old, hold Shift and refresh again (on a Mac, Command + Shift + R).
- **You can't permanently break anything.** Every save is kept, and any earlier version can be brought back.
- **Some boxes can't be left empty.** If a save won't go through, look for a box that needs filling in.
- **The gray text under a box** tells you anything special about it.

## What's in the menu

- **Start here:** this guide.
- **Your page:** every word, photo and button on your website. The sections are numbered 1 to 13 in the same order you see them on the page, top to bottom.
- **Site settings:** the things used in more than one spot (your booking link, email and Facebook), the menu words, the small print, your share picture, your profile links and your ownership codes for Google, Bing and Pinterest.

## Little formatting tricks

- **Coral highlight:** put two stars around words, like `**ACTION!**`. Works in the headline at the top, the How to get noticed heading, the stats heading and paragraph, and the big navy breaks.
- **Coral underline:** put two underscores around words, like `__Pinterest is a search engine__`. Works in the Why Pinterest text.
- **New paragraph:** leave an empty line between paragraphs.
- **New line:** in boxes that say "each line shows as its own line," just press Enter.
- **Big italic questions at the top:** any paragraph in section 1 that ends with a question mark gets that style automatically.

## Lists (packages, questions, pins, stats, testimonials and more)

- **Add one:** use the add button at the end of the list.
- **Remove one:** use the delete button on that item.
- **Change the order:** drag the item up or down. The page shows them in the same order.

## Photos

- Click a photo box to upload a new picture or pick one you already uploaded.
- Use JPG, PNG or WebP. Keep each one under about 500 KB so your page stays fast. Phone photos are often 3 to 5 MB, so shrink them first at squoosh.app (free).
- **Always fill in the description box** under a photo: a few plain words about what's in it. Screen readers read it out loud, and Google and AI tools read it too.
- Sizes that look best: your top photo and About photo tall (portrait), pin examples in the 2:3 Pinterest shape (like 1000 x 1500), testimonial photos square, share picture 1200 x 630.

## Your page, section by section

### Title and description in Google

The first two boxes. **Title in Google** is the blue link people click in search results and the name on the browser tab (best under 60 characters). **Description in Google** is the gray text under it (around 150 characters). Write them the way you'd describe your business to a stranger in one breath.

### 1. Top of the page

- **First line of the headline:** the darker line on its own ("Pinterest Marketing:").
- **Headline:** use two stars for the coral highlight.
- **Supporting text:** the paragraphs under the headline. A paragraph ending in a question mark becomes one of the big italic questions.
- **Main button:** the button text, and where it goes (`BOOKING_URL` means your booking link).
- **Photo** and **Photo description.**

### 2. How to get noticed

The heading, the small line under it, the paragraph, and **The three strips** (the three bars with the light pink behind them). Keep each strip short so it fits on one or two lines.

### 3. Why Pinterest

The heading, the paragraph (two underscores for the coral underline) and the **Points** (each has its own heading and text).

### Big navy breaks

The three full-width navy moments between sections, in order: the first after Why Pinterest, the second after It's perfect for, the third before Packages. Each has a **Big line**, a **Small line under it** (press Enter for a new line), and optional **Pin designs** (add two to show them tilted on either side, or leave it empty).

### 4. It's perfect for

- **Heading** ("It's perfect for:").
- **Left list (navy):** who you work with.
- **Right side heading (coral)** and **Right side list (coral):** what they need in place first.
- **Underlined line** and **Button text** (the button opens your booking link).
- **Line under the heading** is optional and empty for now. Anything you type there appears under the heading.

### 5. The process

The small line above the heading, the heading, each **Step** (title and text) and the button text. There's also an optional small line beside the heading, empty for now.

### 6. Packages

- **Heading**, **Intro line**, **Button text** and the **Note under the packages** (each line shows as its own line).
- Each **Package** has its name, **Price** (type it the way it should show, like $699), **How often** (one time, monthly), **Who it suits**, and **What's included** (one item per line in the list).
- **Highlight this one** gives the card the highlighted look.
- **Tab above the card** adds the little tab (like "New to Pinterest"); **Tab color** makes it navy or coral.
- To change a price, just change the Price box. To add a package, add one to the list; to retire one, delete it.

### 7. Testimonials

The heading, and each **Testimonial**: what they said, their name, their business or role, and an optional square photo with its description. New reviews are worth adding whenever you get them.

### 8. Pin examples

The heading, the intro line, and each **Pin** (the image and what the pin says or shows). Swap in your newest and best work any time.

### 9. Pinterest stats

The heading (two stars for coral), each **Stat** (the number, what it means, and an optional extra line), and the paragraph under the grid. Six stats fill the grid neatly. Pinterest updates its numbers every year or so, so check them against Pinterest's own newsroom or business site about once a year.

### 10. About you

The small line above your name, your name (the heading), your bio (empty line between paragraphs), your photo and its description, and the button under the photo. **Background points** is optional and empty for now; anything you add shows as a short list under your bio.

### 11. Questions

The heading, and each **Question** with its answer. Add **Booking button under the answer** to put a booking button under that answer (leave it empty for none).

### 12. Contact

The heading, the paragraphs, the button, the heading and line inside the box, and how fast you reply. Your email address and Facebook link here come from **Site settings**.

### 13. Bottom of the page

The heading, the paragraphs and the last line. Its button is the email list button, set up in **Email list**.

### Email list

- **Show the email list buttons** shows or hides both buttons (the one at the top of every screen and the one on the last screen).
- **Signup link:** when your email platform is ready, paste your signup page link here. It has to be a page people can open, often called a share link, form link or landing page. Embed code won't work.
- While the link is empty, the buttons scroll down to the last screen.
- **Button text** for each button.

## Site settings

- **Email address** and **Facebook page link:** used in the Contact box and the footer.
- **Menu links:** the words in the menu at the top and in the footer.

- **Small print in the footer.**
- **Your Pinterest profile link** and **Your other profile links:** not shown on the page. They tell Google and AI tools that these profiles and this site are the same business. Fill these in.

## Getting found on Google, Bing and AI

These are current, recommended methods (checked October 2026). Do them once your site is live at kandaceemory.com.

### Already done for you

- Your site is fast, works on phones, and is easy for search engines and AI tools to read.
- It carries behind-the-scenes details Google and AI tools use: your business, your services and prices, your questions and answers, and your testimonials, all pulled from what you type here, so they stay up to date on their own.
- Google, Bing, ChatGPT, Perplexity and other search and AI crawlers are all allowed in.
- There's a map of your site for search engines (your sitemap) and a share picture.

### 1. Google Search Console (free)

This is how you tell Google your site exists and see how people find you, including from Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode.

1. Go to search.google.com/search-console and sign in with your Google account.
2. Click **Add property**, choose **URL prefix**, and enter `https://kandaceemory.com/`.
3. Choose the **HTML tag** method and copy the tag.
4. Here in the editor, open **Site settings**, paste it into **Ownership codes, Google Search Console code**, and click **Save**.
5. Wait about two minutes, go back to Search Console and click **Verify**.
6. In Search Console, open **Sitemaps**, type `sitemap-index.xml` and click **Submit**.

After a big update, you can paste your site address into the search bar at the top of Search Console and click **Request indexing** so Google looks again sooner.

### 2. Bing Webmaster Tools (free)

Bing powers Microsoft Copilot, and it's one of the sources ChatGPT search uses, so this matters for AI more than people think.

1. Go to bing.com/webmasters and sign in.
2. Once Google Search Console is set up, choose **Import from Google Search Console**. It brings your site and sitemap over in one step.
3. If you'd rather not import, add your site, choose the meta tag option, and paste the code into **Ownership codes, Bing Webmaster Tools code**, then Save and click **Verify**.

Bing Webmaster Tools also has an **AI Performance** report that shows when Copilot cites your site.

### 3. Claim your website on Pinterest

You know this one: claiming your site puts your profile on pins from your site and unlocks analytics for them.

1. In Pinterest, go to **Settings**, then **Claimed accounts**, and click **Claim** next to **Websites**.
2. Choose the **HTML tag** option and copy it.
3. Paste it into **Ownership codes, Pinterest claim code**, click **Save**, wait two minutes, then finish the claim in Pinterest.

### 4. What actually gets you found (and cited by AI)

Google says there's no special trick for its AI features: the same things that help in regular search help there. These are the methods that work right now.

- **Answer real questions.** Every time a client or prospect asks you something, add it to your Questions section with a clear, direct answer. AI tools love pulling direct answers.
- **Be specific.** Say exactly who you help and how (Etsy sellers, TPT sellers, bloggers, e-commerce brands), with your real prices and what's included. Specific pages get cited; vague ones don't.
- **Keep your facts the same everywhere.** Your business name, what you do, your prices and your bio should match on your site, Pinterest profile, Facebook and anywhere else you're listed. AI tools trust what they see repeated consistently.
- **Fill in your profile links** in Site settings so search engines connect your profiles to your site.
- **Pin to your own site.** Pins that link to your site (with keyword-rich titles and descriptions, which you already know how to write) send visitors and help people find you.
- **Get mentioned.** Guest posts, podcast interviews, features in other people's newsletters, and helpful answers in groups or communities where your clients hang out (helpful, not salesy) all count. Each mention with a link to your site helps.
- **Add new testimonials** as they come in.
- **Keep things fresh.** Update your stats yearly and your pin examples and questions as your work grows.
- **Check how you show up.** Every month or two, ask ChatGPT, Copilot, Perplexity and Google the kinds of questions your clients ask (like "who can manage Pinterest for my Etsy shop?") and see what comes up. Answers change from day to day, so look for patterns, not one result.

### Good to know

- **Google Business Profile isn't for you, and that's okay.** Google only allows it for businesses that meet customers face to face, and online-only businesses aren't eligible. Trying anyway can get a listing suspended. The testimonials on your site do that job instead, so keep adding them.
- **Skip the old tricks.** Stuffing keywords, buying links, paying for directory listings, or mass-producing AI-written pages no longer work, and some of them are against Google's rules now.
- **Results take time.** New sites usually take a few weeks to a few months to show up well. Consistency wins, just like Pinterest.
